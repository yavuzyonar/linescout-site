Blackjack Basic Strategy: A Plain-Language Starting Guide

Published August 20, 2026 · 8 min read · By the OddsLighthouse Editorial Team

Ask ten blackjack players why they hit or stand on a given hand and you'll usually get ten different guesses. That's a shame, because blackjack is one of the only casino games where the "right" play for every situation has already been worked out mathematically — it's called basic strategy, and it isn't a trick or a system, just the statistically correct decision for each combination of your hand and the dealer's upcard.

What basic strategy actually is

Basic strategy is a set of decision rules, usually printed as a chart, that tells you whether to hit, stand, double down, or split for every possible hand you could be dealt against every possible dealer upcard. It was worked out through computer simulation of millions of hands, so it isn't a guess or a hunch — it's the play that loses the least money (or wins the most) over the long run for that specific situation.

Play it perfectly and the house edge in a typical shoe drops to somewhere around 0.5%, depending on the exact table rules. Play by instinct instead, and that edge commonly climbs past 2%, sometimes considerably more — the gap between "using the chart" and "playing however feels right" is one of the largest skill gaps in any casino game.

A few decisions that trip people up

Some basic strategy plays feel wrong the first time you see them, which is exactly why so many players skip them:

  • Hitting a hard 16 against a dealer's 7 or higher. It feels reckless, but standing loses more often in the long run — the dealer's strong upcard makes standing the worse of two bad options.
  • Splitting 8s against almost anything, including a dealer 10 or Ace. Two hands starting at 8 beat one hand of 16 (one of the worst totals in the game) often enough to make splitting correct even against a strong dealer card.
  • Standing on a soft 18 against a dealer 9, 10, or Ace. "Soft" hands (with an Ace counted as 11) feel safer to hit, but the chart says stand here more often than intuition suggests.
  • Doubling down on 11 against almost any dealer card. Many players are too cautious to put more money on the table here, but it's one of the most reliably profitable plays in the whole chart.

Why table rules change the numbers

Not all blackjack tables are the same game, mathematically speaking. A few rule differences that matter:

  • Blackjack payout: 3:2 is standard; 6:5 tables (increasingly common, especially on single-deck games) meaningfully worsen your odds — this is one of the biggest edge-killers and easy to spot before you sit down.
  • Dealer hits or stands on soft 17: Dealer standing on soft 17 is slightly better for the player than dealer hitting.
  • Number of decks: Fewer decks generally favor the player slightly, all else equal.
  • Doubling and splitting restrictions: Tables that limit which hands you can double down on, or how many times you can split, tighten the game in the house's favor.
A correct basic strategy chart is specific to a given set of table rules. A generic chart gets you most of the way there, but the exact "right" play for a handful of borderline hands shifts depending on deck count and dealer soft-17 rules — worth checking a chart matched to the specific rules on the table you're playing.

Basic strategy is not card counting

These two get confused constantly. Basic strategy is a fixed set of rules you apply the same way every hand, regardless of what's been dealt already — it's legal everywhere and doesn't require tracking anything. Card counting is a separate, more advanced technique that adjusts bet sizing based on which cards have already come out of the shoe; it's mathematically legitimate but far more involved, and many casinos actively discourage it. Basic strategy alone, with no counting at all, is what gets you to that roughly 0.5% baseline edge.

Even played perfectly, basic strategy does not guarantee winning any individual session — it's a long-run edge reduction, not a way to beat the house on a given night.
